I wanted step-by-step instructions for how to learn to draw or draw better. I wanted to start with the basics quite quickly and the aim is just be good at rough or very rough sketching for figures of people.

## Goal

Reach fast, readable **figure sketching** suitable for diagrams and explanation: clear pose, balance, and action—no anatomy detail.

![Image](https://blog.schoolspecialty.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/main7-1200x624.jpg)


![Image](https://jeffkamangara.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/loomis-mannequin.png?w=640)

## Core principle

Draw **action → structure → indication** (never details). If the pose reads at stick-figure level, it will read when refined.

## Tools and cadence

* Pen or single digital brush; no erasing.
* 15–25 min/day.
* Draw figures 10–15 cm tall (too small kills structure).

## Week 1: Gesture (the fastest payoff)

Daily (10–15 min)

1. Line of action: one sweeping curve for spine/energy.
2. Add head as an oval; pelvis as a tilted wedge.
3. Indicate limbs with single lines (shoulder→elbow→wrist; hip→knee→ankle).
4. Stop. 30–60 seconds per pose; do 15–25 poses.

Success criterion: you can tell **what the person is doing** at a glance.

## Week 2: Proportions you actually need

Daily (15–20 min)

1. Use an **8-head** rule loosely: head, ribcage (2), pelvis (1), legs (4).
2. Block torso as two boxes (ribcage + pelvis) with a twist between them.
3. Place joints early (shoulder/hip/knee) before drawing limbs.

Drill: draw 10 standing figures; exaggerate hip/shoulder tilt so balance reads.

## Week 3: Mannequinization (volume without anatomy)

Daily (15–20 min)

1. Limbs as cylinders; hands/feet as simple wedges.
2. Keep overlaps clear (which form is in front).
3. One contour pass only; no shading.

Drill: draw the same pose twice—first as stick, second as mannequin.

## Week 4: Faces, hands, and simplification

Daily (20–25 min)

1. Heads: sphere + jaw; eye line halfway; nose halfway to chin.
2. Hands: mitten → split into thumb + block; fingers as one unit.
3. Clothing: indicate with 2–3 folds that follow action, not detail.

## Five rules for diagram-grade figures

1. Exaggerate action slightly; neutrality reads dead.
2. Show weight: drop a vertical from head—does it land over the foot?
3. Overlap beats outline; avoid tracing the silhouette twice.
4. Limit detail to **one cue** (e.g., a collar or shoe angle).
5. Label if needed; clarity > realism.

## 7-minute drill (daily fallback)

* 3 min: six 30-second gestures.
* 2 min: two mannequins.
* 2 min: one figure with labels (role/action).

![Image](https://line-of-action.com/img/examples/figure/figurestudy-gesture8.png)

## What to avoid early

* Copying photos slowly.
* Muscle anatomy.
* Shading.
* Tiny sketches.
