## Part 2

But is it so?

When we read the lamentation of Epuwer, when we read ... we are hearing the lament of the rich and powerful.

We are hearing the vision created by the early archaeologists who naturally focused on great buildings, monuments, the grand burials. Things that left signs for us to find.

Perhaps it wasn't nearly so bad for the average peasant. Perhaps it was even good?

And it wasn't necessarily sudden either: Rome declined over decades, even centuries. Perhaps the cultural disease was already there when Augustus became emperor. By the time the "sack" came Rome was already a shell of its former self.

So perhaps collapse is neither sudden, nor bad for the 99%, nor does it mean anarchy.

We'd have to temper this. Trade got less complex: you couldn't get the pottery you got before. And maybe with Rome there is some evidence that average health declined (but not by much).
