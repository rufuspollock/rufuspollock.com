### 2026-11-16 · What the French Wars of Religion tell us about today's culture war

- **Link:** https://rufuspollock.com/wars-of-religion-culture-war
- **Companion:** https://rufuspollock.com/french-wars-of-religion ("The French Wars of Religion in ten minutes")
- **Image:** `images/wars-of-religion-culture-war/st-bartholomews-day-massacre-dubois.webp` (public domain)
- **Channels:** X / Bluesky thread, LinkedIn, Life Itself Substack (news.lifeitself.org), Seeds of Renaissance channels (it ties to the metacrisis and Second Renaissance framing).
- **Hook:** The last time a new paradigm rose against an old one, France had forty years of civil war and a century of reversals. Today's culture war, between modernity and postmodernity, has the same shape, and a single election win is an interlude, not the end.
- **Thread sketch:**
  1. 24 August 1572, before dawn: the bells ring and the St Bartholomew's Day Massacre begins. I think it's the best guide to our own culture war. 🧵
  2. The Wars of Religion were a culture war between modernity (Protestants: individual faith, read the book yourself) and premodernity (Catholics: hierarchy, the Church as mediator). Ours is modernity vs postmodernity.
  3. Lesson 1: the rise of the new *manufactures* reaction. The Counter-Reformation and the Jesuits then; traditionalists plus "reactionary modernists" (Musk) now.
  4. Lesson 2: each side polarises internally: moderates vs radicals on both sides.
  5. Lesson 3: it oscillates. The Edict of Nantes, the "we won" moment, was revoked 87 years later.
  6. Lesson 4: the party can lose while the paradigm wins.
  7. So the recent Democratic win is an interlude, not the end. [link]
